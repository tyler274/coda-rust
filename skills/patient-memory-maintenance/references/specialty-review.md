# Problem-specific clinical review

Use the relevant prompts to establish or reassess a patient's problems, even when they are outside
the treating physician's specialty. Preserve important clinical detail while choosing investigative
depth for the current question, evidence and uncertainty. This is not a requirement to fill every
item, copy every encounter or perform a new comprehensive review during a focused update.

Read the supporting sources and keep documented facts, reported history, inference and unknowns
distinct. Assessments of appropriateness, eligibility, targets and intervals are clinical judgment;
use clinical-evidence retrieval when medical-knowledge support is needed. Do not infer treatment use,
clinical stability, adherence or a goals-of-care decision from absent newer documentation.

## Cardiovascular

- **Coronary disease:** preserve dated events and procedures, presentation, documented anatomy,
  lesion severity and interventions or bypass grafts. Retain the antiplatelet plan, intended
  duration and any documented stop date, current supported symptom status and dated ischemic
  evaluation. An old prescription does not confirm treatment is still being taken.
- **Heart failure:** preserve type and supported cause, dated ejection-fraction values and modality,
  functional status, hospitalizations, diuretic regimen and documented target weight. For relevant
  treatment classes, retain doses and documented reasons a therapy is absent, held or limited.
  Distinguish a documented target from a target you recommend based on retrieved evidence or
  qualified model knowledge. Absence alone does not establish an inappropriate omission.
- **Arrhythmias:** retain type and pattern, rate/rhythm strategy, dated ablations or cardioversions,
  and burden or recurrence. For anticoagulation, preserve indication, agent, dose, relevant dated
  renal function, age and weight, documented risk scores and bleeding. Explain the evidence for
  any proposed dose concern rather than treating a code or missing field as a clinical verdict.
- **Devices:** preserve type, documented manufacturer, implant date and indication, latest
  interrogation and findings, monitoring status and relevant delivered therapies or alerts.
- **Valve disease:** identify valve and lesion, severity and dated measurements, symptoms,
  surveillance plan, interventions and any prosthesis-related anticoagulation requirement.
- **Vascular disease:** preserve relevant peripheral arterial disease, dated aortic measurements,
  carotid disease, and thromboembolism with documented provocation and intended treatment duration.
- **Risk factors and care team:** retain dated blood pressure, lipid, glycemic, tobacco and weight
  information that changes judgment, the treating clinician and documented follow-up. Cross-reference
  preventive care and existing problems rather than duplicating them.

Consider unresolved treatment plans, courses beyond their documented duration, unexplained gaps in
therapy or monitoring, conflicting anticoagulation information and missing specialist follow-through.
Distinguish a supported problem from something requiring clarification.

## Oncology

For relevant malignancies, including remission or surveillance, preserve:

- **Diagnosis:** primary site, laterality, histology, grade, presentation and dated method of diagnosis.
- **Stage:** clinical and pathologic stage separately, dated TNM elements and named system/edition.
  Preserve original staging alongside subsequent restaging or recurrence.
- **Biomarkers and genetics:** dated tumor or receptor findings with specimen context and germline
  findings; distinguish completed testing from a recommendation or pending result.
- **Treatment course:** clinically meaningful lines in sequence, intent, regimens, start/stop dates,
  completion, dose changes and reasons for stopping. Preserve relevant surgical margins and nodes,
  and radiation site, dose and fractions when they affect understanding.
- **Response and current disease:** supported response or progression with dated imaging or markers;
  distinguish the last documented state from a current assessment.
- **Toxicity and late effects:** persistent effects that shape care and their monitoring, including
  relevant neurologic, cardiac, endocrine, lymphatic or secondary-cancer concerns.
- **Function and goals:** documented performance scale and date, prognosis discussions, goals of care,
  advance directives and palliative/hospice involvement. These are documented choices, not inferences.
- **Care ownership:** treating specialists, surveillance plan and next documented study or visit.

Consider unresolved imaging, pathology or molecular results, due surveillance, treatment toxicity,
recommended genetic testing not confirmed complete, and survivorship needs with unclear care ownership.
Keep the disease course here and cross-reference preventive surveillance when useful.

## Gastroenterology and hepatology

- **Endoscopy:** retain dated indication, extent, preparation quality, findings and relevant polyp
  number, size, location, pathology and removal completeness. Preserve the endoscopist's documented
  interval and link the surveillance implication to preventive care.
- **Inflammatory bowel disease:** retain type, documented extent/behavior, diagnosis, extraintestinal
  manifestations and surgery. Preserve relevant treatment sequence and reasons for changes, current
  supported regimen, levels/antibodies and dated activity evidence. Include meaningful steroid exposure,
  infection-screening requirements and dysplasia surveillance without assuming completion.
- **Liver disease:** preserve cause, fibrosis/cirrhosis evidence with method and date, documented
  severity scores, dated decompensation and treatment, variceal follow-through, liver surveillance,
  alcohol-related context and transplant evaluation where relevant.
- **Viral hepatitis:** retain diagnostic evidence, treatment and dates, supported response or cure,
  and remaining monitoring.
- **Upper GI disease:** retain supported reflux course and response, Barrett findings, ulcer cause,
  H. pylori testing/treatment/eradication confirmation, and the indication for long-term acid suppression.
- **Bleeding:** preserve dated presentation, identified source, transfusion/intervention and relevant
  antithrombotic or anti-inflammatory exposure and subsequent changes.
- **Pancreaticobiliary disease:** retain episodes, supported causes, dated cyst changes and documented
  surveillance or intervention plans.
- **Functional disorders and nutrition:** preserve the supported diagnosis and workup, treatments and
  response, evaluated alarm features, weight changes, deficiencies and relevant nutrition support.
- **Care team:** retain the responsible specialist and documented follow-up.

Consider surveillance that is due, pathology without a closed plan, eradication not confirmed,
unresolved anemia, therapy-related screening gaps and unsupported continuation of a treatment.
Keep unknown follow-through distinct from known noncompletion and explain what would resolve it.
