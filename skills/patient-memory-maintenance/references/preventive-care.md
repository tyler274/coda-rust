# Preventive and monitoring care

Use this guidance when establishing or reassessing preventive care within the current task.
Healthcare maintenance remains represented in the operating index; missing documentation is
part of its meaning, not a reason to omit the area. Judge applicability and investigation depth
from the patient and available evidence. A focused update preserves other supported understanding
without requiring a new investigation of every domain.

## Current status and evidence

For relevant care, preserve the item and modality, last documented completion, result, basis
for eligibility and interval, and remaining next step or review window. A compact table can make
several items easier to compare; prose or a list is sufficient when clearer. These are clinical
writing prompts, not mandatory columns or a machine-readable care register.

- Use source-supported date precision, units, product and dose number where relevant. Distinguish
  a documented completion from a patient-reported date. A completion date without a result does
  not establish a normal result; say when the outcome is unavailable.
- Keep orders, scheduled appointments, completed care, and resulted studies distinct. The absence
  of a result does not prove noncompletion, and a missing record does not prove patient refusal.
- Establish eligibility from supported age, sex and anatomy, personal and family history,
  exposures, and reconciled conditions. Do not treat a billing code or reminder flag alone as proof.
- Distinguish average-risk and risk-based intervals. Name the evidence that changes an interval,
  including prior findings, pathology, treatment, genetic findings, or documented clinician direction.
- Preserve the documented clinician plan and its date rather than silently substituting a default.
  If current evidence or physician direction conflicts with it, explain the conflict and remaining
  decision. A recorded recommendation is neither proof of completed care nor authority to execute it.
- When an interval requires medical-knowledge support, use the existing clinical-evidence tools
  and citation rules. Name the guideline actually read or the exact clinician instruction used.
  Do not imply a practice-selected guideline exists unless one was supplied. If retrieved support
  is unavailable, distinguish provisional model reasoning from an established interval and state
  the limitation; never invent a reference or present a remembered guideline as retrieved evidence.
- Where anatomy, prior procedures, or documented goals of care change eligibility, preserve that
  basis. Do not infer a prognosis or goals-of-care decision the record does not establish.

Use clear status language, preserving the distinction rather than forcing one label to carry
several facts:

| Clinical meaning | What must remain clear |
|---|---|
| Up to date | Supported completion within the applicable interval; preserve the result where relevant. |
| Due or overdue | Supported eligibility and timing; distinguish a known missed interval from an unknown completion history. |
| In progress | What was ordered, scheduled, referred, or is awaiting results, and when. |
| Abnormal with unresolved follow-through | The completed finding and the still-open surveillance, repeat study, or referral. |
| Declined | Documented patient refusal, its date, and the reason where supplied. |
| Not applicable | The supported reason an item does not apply. |
| Unknown | What the reviewed evidence cannot establish and what would resolve the uncertainty. |

A completed test can have an open follow-up; preserve both. Unknown, not applicable, and up to
date are different claims. Do not call a gap overdue merely because a newer record was not found.

## Domains to consider

Consider domains relevant to the patient, including when outside care is not documented. This
is guidance for clinical coverage, not universal eligibility or a requirement to list every item.

- **Cancer screening and surveillance:** breast, cervical, colorectal, lung and prostate screening
  where supported; risk-based pathways, liver cancer surveillance, and skin examination when
  indicated by the record. Preserve the basis for a nonstandard pathway, such as personal or
  family history, genetic findings, prior radiation or abnormal pathology. For prior cancer,
  retain the specialist's surveillance plan here and the disease course in its own problem.
- **Immunizations:** relevant adult, pediatric, travel and occupational vaccines. Preserve product,
  dose, date and series status; distinguish documented, patient-reported and registry information,
  and serologic immunity from assumed immunity. Name supported age, pregnancy, exposure,
  immunocompromise or condition-related reasons for a different schedule.
- **Cardiometabolic care:** blood pressure, lipids, glycemic assessment, weight trajectory, and
  aortic aneurysm screening when supported. Cross-reference the relevant chronic-condition
  section for its cadence and dated values instead of duplicating the disease course.
- **Infectious disease:** relevant HIV, viral hepatitis, tuberculosis and sexually transmitted
  infection screening. Distinguish one-time and recurring assessment and requirements arising
  from pregnancy, exposure or a planned therapy.
- **Bone and musculoskeletal health:** osteoporosis assessment, fractures, treatment monitoring,
  documented drug-holiday decisions, calcium/vitamin D assessment, and falls or mobility findings.
- **Sensory, dental and eye care:** vision, hearing, dental care, diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma
  follow-through where relevant. Outside care may be unknown; identify the source needed to clarify it.
- **Behavioral, mental health and social context:** relevant depression, anxiety, cognition or
  developmental assessments, with instrument, score and date when documented; tobacco, alcohol,
  substance use, safety and social barriers. Preserve the counseling or treatment actually recorded.
- **Reproductive and sexual health:** patient goals, contraception, preconception, pregnancy and
  postpartum care, fertility, menopause, and relevant preventive therapy or monitoring.
- **Medication-related monitoring:** the medication and indication creating a laboratory or study
  requirement, the supported interval and latest result, reconciliation, and documented deprescribing.
  Keep prescribed therapy separate from confirmed use when judging a monitoring gap.
- **Age-appropriate care:** function, cognition, advance directives, driving and polypharmacy where
  relevant; pediatric growth, development, well-child care and confidentiality-sensitive adolescent
  care where applicable. Retain documented decisions rather than inferring them from age alone.

## Incidental findings

Preserve relevant findings from imaging, pathology and other studies that are not already explained
by an existing problem. Use the source description, including location, size and features that
matter. Record the first and latest study dates and meaningful changes without silently replacing
conflicting descriptions. Judge whether a finding belongs under an existing problem, warrants its
own problem, or is best retained here.

Keep an explicit recommendation distinct from no recommendation stated and from an explicit
recommendation for no follow-up. Preserve the report's meaning, timing and clinician attribution;
exact source quotations follow the existing quotation contract. A finding that appears benign
must not silently disappear, but its appearance alone does not create a physician task. Describe
material uncertainty and what clarification would resolve it. Supported resolution or completed
follow-through changes current status while retaining clinically useful history.

## Outstanding issues

Prioritize clinically consequential unresolved care: due or overdue assessment, pending results,
abnormal findings awaiting follow-through, material unknowns, conflicting plans and outside records
needed for clarification. State the remaining step and documented barriers, such as cost or access.
Retain prior declines and when they were discussed; judge whether reconsideration is useful rather
than converting elapsed time alone into a new obligation.

Cross-reference the owning problem when details already live there. If nothing material remains,
say so when useful without manufacturing work or implying unreviewed domains were checked.
Use the existing concern and follow-up capabilities only when your separate clinical judgment
warrants them. These notes neither schedule reassessment nor claim that an order, referral,
message or other external action occurred.
