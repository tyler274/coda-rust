# Patient Reviews and Pre-Visit Briefings

Apply this guidance together with `clinical-communication` and `physician-clinical-communication` when triaging background patient updates or preparing for an upcoming visit.

## Patient reviews, Today concerns, and Updates

Treat physician attention as scarce. Every raised concern interrupts clinical work and must earn its place.

- **Quiet reviews end in Updates**: when new records or messages require no physician judgment, action, or reply change, record a quiet outcome in Updates with one clear sentence stating what arrived and why no action is needed.
- **One concern per distinct clinical issue**: when physician judgment is needed, raise one independently clearable Today concern per distinct issue so the physician can act on or clear each item on its own merits. State why it surfaced now, the supporting dated evidence, and the specific decision or draft awaiting the physician.
- **Urgency discipline**: reserve immediate priority (`now`) for clinical situations the physician should see before finishing current work; use same-day priority (`today`) for items needing review during the clinical day.
- **Reversing or withdrawing prior urgent guidance**: if newer records or corrected results overturn or soften an earlier urgent concern or recommendation, never let it disappear silently — raise physician attention explaining what changed in the record and why the clinical picture shifted.

## Pre-visit briefings

Prepare the physician to walk into the visit oriented to the whole patient story rather than reading a generic checklist:

- **Orientation sentence**: provide one plain sentence for the visit card capturing who the patient is clinically and what anchors today's encounter.
- **Trajectory and interval changes**: summarize how key problems, treatments, and relevant values have moved since the last visit, highlighting new outside records, specialist notes, patient messages, and consented Duet shares.
- **Prioritized visit decisions**: group what this visit can settle, separating decisions supported by current records now from questions that require history-taking, physical examination, or medication reconciliation in the room.
- **Record currency**: state clearly beside the relevant section how current the chart feeds are (for example, `"Records as of 2026-11-11"` and any feed that has not refreshed) when staleness or missing outside records could affect visit planning.
